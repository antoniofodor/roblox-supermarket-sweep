Supermarket Sweep MVP

The smallest version that can tell us whether the game is fun: your shopping frenzy (the list, ramming and stealing), plus the closing shutters and the checkout queue. Big Chonk, a giant waiting outside to be fed, turns the rounds into a 5-round elimination match with one winner. Everything else waits until a playtest says the core is worth building on.

What the MVP has to prove

Every feature in the MVP is there to answer one of these five questions. If a playtest says no to one of them, fix that before adding anything.

1. Do players gamble on the shutters?Some players should stay for one more item and get caught. If nobody ever does, the shutters are too easy to beat. If most players do, they're too harsh.
2. Is ramming fun on both ends?Ramming someone should feel great, and getting rammed should feel fair rather than cheap.
3. Does the closing rush happen?Queues at the tills, last-second dashes, someone getting shut in. That's the moment people will clip.
4. Do they press "again"?The real test is players starting the next round without being asked.
5. Can a new player learn it in one round?No tutorial and no explaining. If they need telling, the rules are too complicated.
6. Does Big Chonk make the list matter?Players should talk about him, read his shouts and fight over his cravings. If he's just a scoreboard with a face, the character isn't doing its job.

  2. You get a shopping list of 6 foods when the doors open. They're staples, the food he needs: milk, eggs, bread, fruit and so on. A staple is 2 yum, and 4 yum if it's on your list.
  3. Snacks are 3 yum (crisps, cookies, soda). He loves them. They're never on a list, but they're worth more than any staple that isn't on yours.
  4. Yuck food is -2 yum: Brussels sprouts, canned peas, spinach, and toilet roll. It's never on a list, and a till scans it against you, so it's ammunition: throw it at someone, or get it into a rival's trolley. It sits on the shelves right beside the food you want, so you pick some up on the way. Toilet roll leaves a streamer of paper wherever it flies. (Toilet roll is in now; the yuck foods come with Big Chonk.)
  5. You push a trolley. Walk up to a shelf and press E to drop an item in. It holds 20 items, and you move a little slower the fuller it gets.
  6. You can let go of your trolley (Q) to run faster and carry 2 items, one in each hand. When you grab it again, you keep the newest in your free hand and the other goes into the trolley. While it's parked, anyone can steal from it (hold E).
  7. Your trolley is a pile, not a list. The screen shows how full it is, never what's in it. Things land in it that you didn't want: thrown in, shaken off a shelf, knocked out of someone else's trolley, or tumbling in when you scrape a shelf. You only hear that SOMETHING landed.
  8. Dig (R, or the DIG button) to look straight down into your basket and pull out what's on top into your hands. Your own hands show at the bottom of the view holding what you pulled out, highlighted. Pushing takes one hand, so you hold one item while you push and two with your trolley parked. When your hands are full, throw one or tap it to put it back on top of the pile before you dig out more. While you dig you stand still, and anyone can ram you or rob you. Dig, or keep shopping: that's the choice.
  9. Run your trolley into someone else's and the top item flies out onto the floor for anyone to grab.
  10. Throw an item (F, or the THROW button) along a real arc toward where you aim: the mouse, or the middle of the screen on a phone. You throw what's in your hand, or else the top of your pile, whatever it is. Dig something out first to throw it on purpose. Whoever it hits is knocked off balance for 1.5 seconds: slowed, unable to grab or sprint. The item lands on the floor for anyone to take.
  11. Sprint your trolley into a shelf (or a fridge, or the fruit table) and it shakes: 1 to 3 of its items fall into the aisle for anyone to grab.
  12. Flash sale for the last 30 seconds (1:30 to closing). One golden turkey worth 10 yum appears at the right-hand end of the middle aisle, under a beam of light, just after the store has told everyone to head for the tills. Grab it and get it through a till before the shutters shut, or lose it with everything else.
  13. Sprint by holding Shift (or the SPRINT button). Holding drains a meter that lasts 3 seconds; letting go refills it, so you can sprint in short bursts.
  14. Shelves don't restock. Once the milk is gone it's gone, so popular items get fought over and the store empties as the round goes on.
  15. The tills open at 1:00, halfway through. Nobody can grab two things and leave: everyone shops for the first half.
  16. Check out at a till. Items scan one by one. Whatever has scanned is safe. There are 3 tills, so at closing time there's a queue.
  17. The shutters come down over the last 20 seconds. Anything not scanned by 2:00 is lost. Finishing early earns a bonus for every second left.
  18. Most yum out wins. Top 3 go on the podium, then the next round starts automatically.

Big Chonk and the elimination match

Players aren't shopping for themselves any more. A huge, hungry giant called Big Chonk waits outside the store, and every player gets a list of what he wants to eat. After each round he eats what everyone brought, and whoever fed him worst gets eaten. Five rounds, one winner.

Underneath, the round is the one you already have: the list becomes his cravings, the till is how food gets out to him, and the results screen becomes his mood meter. The playtest still answers the same questions about the round, plus one more: does a character make players care about their list?

The match

  1. A match is 5 rounds. Each round is the 2-minute shopping round as it is now, then the feeding.
  2. Chonk scores what each player brings him. His score is yum: food on your list counts double, snacks count 3, any other staple 2, and yuck food counts against you. Shut in by the shutters means you bring him nothing.
  3. Rounds 1 to 4 each eliminate the players who fed him worst. The cuts are sized so that exactly 3 players reach round 5, with most of the cuts late, so a bad first round isn't the end.
  4. Round 5 is the final. The 3 survivors are ranked 1st, 2nd and 3rd on a podium. Only 1st is the winner: crowned, and carried around by Chonk.
  5. Ties go to whoever checked out first.
  6. With 3 players or fewer, nobody is eliminated and round 5 decides the podium. In Studio, the admin start runs the match solo.
  7. Players who join mid-match wait in the stands until the next match.

During the round

Yuck food: things he hates

  • Chonk always hates the same few: Brussels sprouts, canned peas, spinach, and toilet roll. Each is -2 yum. They never change, so a child learns them once. This replaces the earlier idea of 2 new hated foods every round.
  • Stocked next to the good stuff: sprouts beside the fruit, peas beside the crisps and cookies, spinach in the fridges beside the steak. Everyone passes yuck on the way to what they want, so everyone has ammunition.
  • Sabotage, two ways: a thrown item that lands in someone's trolley goes into it, and holding E on a rival's parked trolley with yuck in your hand plants it there (the reverse of stealing). Either way, the till scans it against them.
  • Feeding him one makes him gag and spit it back. The counter is digging it out of your trolley before you check out.
  • Not in the mood: each round he's tired of one food he normally likes ("NO PIZZA TODAY!"), announced at the doors and shown on the HUD. It's on nobody's list that round and counts -1 yum: a small minus, less than yuck.

Cravings change

  • At 0:40, Chonk suddenly craves one kind of food. For the rest of the round it's worth triple to everyone. (Moved from 1:00 so it doesn't land on top of the tills opening.)
  • Announced by Chonk himself, and the craving is marked on the HUD and over its shelf.
  • Everyone changes direction at once, which is the point.
  • The flash sale stays in the last 30 seconds (1:30), so the craving owns the middle of the round and the turkey owns the end.

He shouts at you

  • Chonk stands outside the front window, visible from inside the store.
  • Speech bubbles over him and lines on the HUD, by name: "SAM! WHERE'S MY CHEESE?"
  • He gets angrier as the clock runs down. In the last 20 seconds he bangs on the window and the glass shakes.
  • He reacts to what happens: cheering a big ram, groaning when someone grabs his hated food.

After you're eliminated

  • Built first: spectating. Eaten players (and anyone who joins mid-match) watch the survivors: the camera follows one, and arrows switch between them.
  • Still recommended: eliminated players don't just watch. They come back into the store as Chonk's pests: they can't shop, but they can throw yuck food at the survivors.
  • Kids don't sit through 10 minutes of watching, and pests make the late rounds more chaotic, not less.
  • The alternative is spectating from the stands. To decide before building round 2 of the match.

The feeding (after every round)

  1. Everyone lines up on a stage in front of Chonk, outside the store, each holding their shopping bag.
  2. Chonk grabs each bag and dumps it into his mouth, and everyone waits for his reaction: a happy belly drum, a shrug, or a gag and spit for hated food.
  3. His mood meter is the score. Over each player's head, a meter runs from angry through content to love. The needle moves as each item goes in: up for food he wants, up a lot for list food, and back down with a gag for food he hates. Where it stops is that player's result for the round. The exact value shows small beside it, but the meter is what players read.
  4. The safe players go fast (about 1.5 s each). The players in danger are saved for last, for suspense: their needle wobbles around the edge of angry before it settles.
  5. Then he eats the eliminated players. A cartoon gulp, and he spits them out into the stands. Nothing they own is lost; it's a joke, not a punishment.
  6. After round 5, the podium: 3rd, 2nd, then the winner, who Chonk lifts up and carries.

Throwing items

Added after the first playtest, and reworked after the second: the first version locked on and mostly dropped the item at your feet. Throwing gives players a way to dump extras from a full trolley, and a way to slow a rival down so somebody else gets to a scarce item first.

How it works

  • What you throw: the item in your hand if you're holding one, otherwise the top of your pile. To throw something specific, dig it out first.
  • You aim it. It flies in a real arc toward your mouse (or the middle of the screen on a phone), and hits whoever it passes close to. A shopper within 12° of your aim gets light aim assist, with a lead for where they're running, because hitting a moving target on a phone is hard.
  • A hit knocks them off balance for 1.5 s: slowed to 40%, a wobble and dizzy stars, no grabbing and no sprinting. It doesn't take any of their items; that's what ramming does.
  • The thrown item lands on the floor for anyone to grab, the thrower included.
  • The client sends only an aim direction. The server clamps it, flies the item and decides who's hit; every screen draws the same arc.

Guardrails

  • 5 s hit immunity, counted for the player being hit. A crowd can't keep one child knocked over by taking turns, however many people are throwing. A cooldown on the thrower alone wouldn't stop that.
  • Tills are safe. Nobody can be hit while standing on a till pad, so a queue can't be wrecked at closing. Ramming and stealing are off there too.
  • Throwing costs an item. It's cheap, but a player who throws all round goes home with less, so constant throwing doesn't pay.
  • 2 s between throws so it never turns into spam.

Ramming and throwing do different jobs

Ramming is up close, needs speed, and takes an item. Throwing works at range, costs you an item, and slows a shopper. That split keeps either one from making the other pointless. If playtests show everyone throwing and nobody ramming, lengthen the throw cooldown first.

Shaking shelves

Ram a shelf hard enough and it wobbles and drops a few of its own items onto the floor. It fits the chaos and costs little to build. Planned for milestone 4.

How it works

  • Sprint into a shelf with your trolley and the shelf section you hit shakes. It needs a sprint, not a plain push: otherwise just driving up to a shelf to grab something would knock its stock off every time.
  • 1 to 3 items from that section fall into the aisle you're in. Only items actually on that section, so it never adds stock: shelves still never restock.
  • Fallen items are anybody's. You shook them loose, but a shopper standing in the aisle can scoop them first. That makes it a gamble, not a shortcut.
  • The shake is drawn on each player's screen from one message; the server only decides what falls.

Things to watch

  • One shake per section every ~6 s. Without it, a player parks at the steak and rams it empty.
  • It empties the store faster. Scarcity is what makes the fights matter, so check the store isn't bare by the 2-minute mark.
  • A messy floor is the point. If grabbing off the floor ever beats grabbing off shelves, items need to fall further away.
  • Later, maybe: a falling item bonks whoever is underneath, using the same off-balance rules as a throw.

Art direction

A chaotic, goofy, low-poly look, with custom shopper characters instead of standard Roblox avatars. Nothing about it is needed to find out whether the round is fun, so it comes after the playtest. The grey box answers the fun question.

Why it works

  • Goofy matches the game. Wobbly shoppers, oversized trolleys and items flying everywhere read as funny rather than mean, which suits players knocking each other over.
  • Low poly is cheap twice: quick to make, and light on phones, where most of the audience plays.
  • It looks like its own game in a thumbnail, where most Roblox games look like Roblox.
  • Characters become the shop. A granny in curlers, a toddler, a bodybuilder, a dad in socks and sandals: shoppers and their outfits are the cosmetic catalogue.

The costs

  • Players lose their own avatars, which many care about. Show their name on their shopper, and let them choose and dress their shopper, so it still feels like theirs.
  • Custom characters need their own animations. Build them on the standard R15 skeleton so Roblox's walk, run and emotes still work, and only the look is custom.
  • Bigger or odd shapes change the collisions that ramming, throwing and the tills were tuned on. Keep the collision body the standard size and make only the visible model goofy.
  • It's the biggest art job in the plan. Do the store and items in low poly first, then the characters, once the playtest says the game is worth it.

The store

One store, built for 8 to 12 players, about 160 by 150 studs. There are two blocks of six parallel aisles with a cross aisle between them. Each aisle is about 13 studs wide, enough for two trolleys to pass. The front wall, tills and lobby stay where they were; the store grew backwards, so the fridges are the longest run from the tills.

Why this layout

  • Expensive items at the back (fridges, flash-sale spot) and the tills at the front, so the best stuff is the furthest run from the exit.
  • One way in, and the only way out is through the tills, so the queue can't be skipped.
  • The tills sit in a bottleneck by the exit, so trolleys pile up there at closing.

Items (12 kinds, ~60 on the shelves)

  • Staples, 2 yum (4 on your list): bananas, apples, bread, cereal, and milk, cheese, eggs, steak in the back fridges. The only food on lists.
  • Snacks, 3 yum: crisps, cookies, soda. Never on a list.
  • Yuck food, -2 yum: toilet roll now (thrown first, leaves a paper streamer); Brussels sprouts, canned peas and spinach with Big Chonk, each stocked beside food players want.
  • Flash sale, 10 yum: one golden turkey for the last 30 seconds
  • Simple coloured box shapes with a picture on the front are enough for the MVP.

Tech notes

Setup (from your new-game checklist)

  • Rojo project, so the repo is the game.
  • One Config, one Remotes, one Theme module.
  • Admin console on day one, checked on the server against an allowlist.
  • No DataStore in the MVP at all: nothing to save, nothing to break.
  • StreamingEnabled off: one small map.

Who decides what

  • The server owns items, lists, trolley contents, scores and the clock.
  • The trolley is welded in front of the player's character, so pushing it is smooth on their own screen.
  • The server decides a ram from the two trolleys' speed and contact, never from what a client says.
  • A throw sends no target: the server picks who's hit from its own positions, and owns the stumble and the immunity clock.
  • Items on the floor and on shelves are simple anchored parts the server moves.
